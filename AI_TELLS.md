# Signs that something looks AI-made

A running list of things that make a page, text or design look like AI made it. Each entry says what the sign is, why it gives the game away, and what to do instead. New entries come from examples Thabiso points out.

The goal isn't to hide that AI was involved. It's to make the result feel like a person made deliberate choices.

## The general idea

Most of these come down to one thing: **AI decorates information instead of just saying it.** Where a person would write a sentence, AI adds a label, a badge, a pill, an icon or a little caption around it. Each one looks tidy on its own, but together they make a page feel assembled from a template instead of written by someone.

A useful test: *would a person writing this by hand bother to add this element?* If the honest answer is no, leave it out.

## Confirmed

### 1. Em dashes ( — )

**Sign:** Long dashes used to join clauses, add asides or set up a punchline ("No install — just scan a QR code").

**Why it looks AI:** AI uses em dashes far more often than most people do. Many people don't even know how to type one, so a page full of them is an instant giveaway.

**Instead:** Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets. Usually two short sentences read better anyway.

### 2. Small labels stacked above a title

**Sign:** A tiny uppercase line above a heading, sometimes with a badge next to it, before you get to the actual title. On this page it was "APP PORTFOLIO" plus a "WORK IN PROGRESS" badge above the name, and "THE WAY I WORK" above "How I build with AI".

**Why it looks AI:** These are called eyebrows or kickers. They're a fixed habit of AI-generated landing pages. A person writes the title and starts.

**Instead:** Let the heading stand alone. If the information matters (like "work in progress"), say it in a normal sentence somewhere sensible.

### 3. Facts shown as pills or chips instead of plain text

**Sign:** Properties shown as a row of rounded tag-like boxes: `● Web app live` `Flutter · Firebase` `English · Thai · Chinese`.

**Why it looks AI:** It turns plain information into UI decoration. People describing their own work would just write it down.

**Instead:** A plain sentence: "The web app is live and the Android app is in testing. Built with Flutter and Firebase, available in English, Thai and Chinese."

### 4. Patterned or textured backgrounds

**Sign:** A subtle dot grid, grain, noise or mesh gradient behind the page.

**Why it looks AI:** It's added to make a page feel "designed" without it meaning anything.

**Instead:** A flat background colour.

### 5. Trendy font pairings

**Sign:** A stylish display serif for headings combined with a grotesk sans for body text. This page first used Fraunces with Hanken Grotesk. Other usual suspects: Instrument Serif, Playfair Display, Space Grotesk, DM Sans, Inter.

**Why it looks AI:** AI design tools reach for the same handful of "tasteful" Google Fonts, so they've become a fingerprint.

**Instead:** One solid, professional family used for everything, with hierarchy done through size and weight. This page now uses IBM Plex Sans.

## Suspected (not confirmed yet)

Things I (Claude) suspect, to check with Thabiso before treating them as rules:

- **Arrows after links** ("Try the web app →", "How I work with AI ↓"). Removed from the page for now.
- **Numbered steps styled as "01 02 03".** Removed along with the old process grid.
- **Coloured status dots** (green dot for "live", amber for "in progress"). Removed with the pills.
- **Groups of three everywhere:** three features, three adjectives, three bullet points.
- **"Not X, but Y" phrasing** and short punchy sentences used as slogans ("No install, no account.").
- **Bold lead-in labels** at the start of a line ("**What's next:** ...").
