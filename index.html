SportsPort

Live tournament management for amateur badminton.

  • Web app live · Android in testing
  • Flutter · Firebase
  • English · Thai · Chinese

Try the web app Watch the demos Full case study

The app

Amateur badminton tournaments in Chiang Mai run on Google Sheets. Players refresh a spreadsheet between matches to find out when and where they play next. SportsPort keeps the sign-up flow organizers already use and adds what spreadsheets can't do: live standings on every phone, real-time match tracking, and a schedule that adjusts as the day runs.

  • Imports the Google Forms registration sheet directly
  • Group + knockout, Swiss + knockout, or straight knockout
  • Results update on every device instantly
  • Remaining-time estimate based on actual match pace
  • Players join by scanning a QR code — no install, no account

What it shows about my experience

  • A full product: web dashboard and mobile app from one codebase
  • Real-time data, user accounts, roles and permissions
  • Tested end to end on two real past tournaments
  • Brand, landing page and app-store listing done myself
  • Idea to working web app in about X weeks

Three decisions that shaped it

  1. Two screens, one codebase

    Organizers work from a laptop, players from a phone. Instead of one layout stretched to fit both, each gets an interface designed for how they actually use it, sharing the same logic underneath.

  2. Keep the "one page" feeling

    Organizers coming from spreadsheets look for the single sheet that shows everything. A map view and a printable PDF give them exactly that, alongside the interactive app.

  3. Nothing in the way of joining

    At a casual Saturday tournament, nobody installs an app. So you don't have to: scan a QR code, see your matches. Accounts only when you want to do more.

ConvoGo

A face-to-face translator for two people and one phone.

  • In the App Store & Google Play
  • Flutter · Cloudflare Workers
  • iOS · Android

App Store link Google Play link

The app

Two people standing face to face who don't share a language. One holds the phone, both speak, and the app shows what was said in both languages as a simple chat. No record buttons, no robot voice reading things out — just switch speaker and keep talking.

It started as a fun experiment and turned into a complete app.

  • No buttons per turn: switching speaker is the only action
  • Built to use with one thumb
  • Reading, not listening: works in noisy places

What it shows about my experience

  • Published on both the App Store and Google Play
  • Speech recognition and translation working together live
  • A small backend that keeps API keys off the phone
  • Designing by removing things, not adding them
  • Built in about X weeks

Zouzou

One app for a personal trainer and her clients.

  • In progress
  • Flutter · Firebase
  • Chinese · English

The app

Built for a personal trainer in Chiang Mai whose clients are almost all Chinese. Trainer and clients use the same app: she plans workouts and manages session packs, they follow their workouts, track progress and book sessions.

  • Two roles, one shared app
  • Built around session packs, how trainers here actually sell
  • Chinese-first, not translated afterwards
